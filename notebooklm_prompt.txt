## Context of my paper (use this to judge relevance)

Title: "Disentangling Scale from Targeted Pretraining Depth: Testing the
Less-Is-More Hypothesis for Bengali Mathematical Reasoning"

I fine-tuned seven language model backbones (0.6B–4B parameters) with LoRA on
~1000 curated Bengali mathematical reasoning traces from the Ganit dataset, in
a LIMO-style minimal-data elicitation setup, and evaluated them on a 500-problem
held-out Bengali maths benchmark before and after fine-tuning.

My backbones fall into two groups:
- Bengali-adapted models: TigerLLM-1B (Gemma3 base, ~10M curated Bengali
  textbook tokens) and TituLM-1B/3B (Llama-3.2 base, ~37B general Bengali
  tokens), including instruction-tuned variants.
- General multilingual models with no Bengali-specific pretraining:
  Qwen3-0.6B, Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-4B.

My main findings, which each source should be assessed against:

1. Model scale determined performance; targeted Bengali pretraining depth did
   not. Qwen3 scaled 2% -> 14% -> 45% accuracy across 0.6B/1.7B/4B (every step
   significant). A 3,700x difference in Bengali pretraining between TigerLLM
   (10M tokens) and TituLM (37B tokens) produced no significant difference
   (p = 0.553). Qwen3-4B, with no Bengali-specific pretraining, beat the best
   Bengali-adapted model by 12x.

2. LIMO-style minimal-data elicitation produced no significant accuracy change
   on any of the seven backbones, including Qwen3-4B where LIMO's stated
   precondition of latent capability clearly holds (45% baseline).

3. Elicitation reliably changed output FORM without changing capability:
   termination behaviour, output length, and adopted formatting all shifted
   significantly, while accuracy did not. Bengali-character adherence fell
   significantly in 5 of 7 models.

4. Training on difficulty-matched (easier) data improved training-set fit but
   significantly WORSENED held-out accuracy (p = 0.0385) — successful
   in-distribution learning transferred negatively.

5. Qwen3's tokenizer requires ~3.1x more tokens than TituLM's extended-vocabulary
   tokenizer for identical Bengali text, which determines what can be trained on
   consumer hardware.
